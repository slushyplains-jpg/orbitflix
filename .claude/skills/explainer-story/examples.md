# Worked examples

Two approved scripts, labeled by the job each sentence does, and one rejected script with what was wrong. Copy the pattern, not the words.

## The Ritual (2017), approved, 233 words, 56.5 s

| Job | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Hook: wound as a shocking image | Luke hid behind a shelf while his best friend was beaten to death, and he did nothing. |
| Setup: time jump, who, why they are here | Six months later, four old friends hike the mountains of northern Sweden, the trip the dead man always wanted. |
| Problem | Then Dom wrecks his knee, and every step is agony. |
| Choice that causes everything | So they leave the trail and cut through the forest, to cut the journey in half. |
| Escalation 1: concrete image | Deep in the trees, they find an elk, gutted and hung high in the branches. |
| Punch (short) | No bear could have put it there. |
| Detail that adds dread | Strange symbols are carved into the trunks all around it. |
| Escalation 2: place | When a storm traps them, they shelter in an empty cabin. |
| Image | Upstairs waits a figure made of twigs, with no head and antlers for hands. |
| Escalation 3: it touches them | That night they all wake up screaming, and Luke finds fresh wounds on his chest. |
| Wrong detail | One of them is found kneeling in front of the figure, as if he has been praying to it. |
| False hope | They follow a path, because a path means people. |
| Hope breaks | It leads to an old tent, abandoned, with its owner's shoes still inside. |
| Question the viewer is already asking | Who walks away from a tent without their shoes? |
| Threat glimpsed | Something huge moves between the trees. |
| The wound returns (emotional turn) | Then the blame comes out. |
| Pay-off of the hook | Dom calls Luke a coward, who walked out of that shop without a drop of blood on him. |
| Turn: the threat strikes | In the night, Hutch vanishes into the dark, screaming. |
| Cliffhanger: specific, open | And whatever took him has been following them since that cabin. |

## Cloverfield (2008), approved, 229 words, 60.2 s

| Job | Sentence |
|---|---|
| Hook: the film's biggest image | The night before he moves to Japan, the head of the Statue of Liberty comes crashing down his street. |
| Rewind to setup | Earlier that night, Rob was at his own farewell party. |
| Wound | Then Beth walks in with another man. They slept together, and Rob never called her. They fight, and she leaves. |
| Disaster starts | Minutes later, the whole building shakes and the lights go out. The news reports a tanker overturned by the Statue of Liberty. |
| Scale | From the roof, they watch the city explode. Something taller than the buildings walks between them, and a skyscraper falls. |
| Plan | The plan is to cross the Brooklyn Bridge and get out. |
| The wound calls | On the bridge, Rob's phone rings. It is Beth, crying, trapped in her apartment, saying she cannot move. |
| Cost | Then a giant tail smashes through the cables, and his brother is gone. |
| Bigger threat | On a store television, the army fires at the creature, and smaller creatures swarm the soldiers. |
| Turn (three short beats) | The whole city is running away from Midtown. Beth lives in Midtown. So while everyone runs from the monster, Rob walks straight toward it. |
| Escalations | They take the subway tunnels, until something skitters out of the dark and bites their friend. At an army hospital, her eyes start bleeding, and medics rush her away. |
| Clock | The last helicopter leaves at six, before they bomb the city. |
| Cliffhanger | And when Rob finally finds Beth's tower, it is leaning on the building next to it. |

## Rejected: the old Ritual script, 203 words, 40.9 s

> Four friends set out on a hiking trip in Sweden to honor their friend Robert, who would have loved this place. They lose their way deep in the forest, and the trip goes wrong quickly. ... Phil insists it was all a nightmare, but the others know better. Gayle's name comes up again and again ... The creatures are called Jotunn, and they hang their victims from the trees. ... Someone promises that the creatures will not kill them, and the forest answers with another roar.

What was wrong:
- Opens with a summary, not an image. Nobody stops scrolling for "four friends set out on a hiking trip".
- No protagonist and no wound. "The group" does everything, so nobody is worth caring about.
- Vague events: "goes wrong quickly", "something is waiting", "the forest keeps growling".
- Dialogue retold from subtitles: "Phil insists", "Gayle's name comes up", "someone asks". These confuse anyone who has not seen the film.
- Spoils the reveal (the creature's name, the sacrifice) and ends on a template line.
- Too short.
