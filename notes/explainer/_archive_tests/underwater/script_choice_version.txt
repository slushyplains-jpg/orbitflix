At the bottom of the Mariana Trench, Norah shuts a door on her own screaming coworkers, because if she doesn't, the ocean crushes everyone. And that is the easiest choice she makes in this entire movie. Something has torn their drilling station apart, every escape pod is already gone, and the reactor is about to melt. So the captain's plan sounds insane. They put on pressure suits and walk a mile across the pitch-black seafloor to another station. Before they even reach the bottom, a cracked helmet implodes with a man still inside it. Then something bursts out of a dead man's back. It has no eyes, and it hunts by their lights. Paul, a research assistant who fled in his bathrobe, carries a stuffed bunny named Lil Paul and jokes the whole way. In a tight pipe, his line tangles, and something drags him under. The last thing he screams is for someone to take Lil Paul. Whatever has him rips him straight out of his suit. Norah knows that kind of loss. She once let her fiancé dive alone, and she never found him. Then the captain, tangled beside her, shouts at her to let go before the pressure kills them both. She can't. What she doesn't know yet is that the last station is full of these things, asleep on the ceiling. And when she finally reaches the escape pods, the computer says one of them is broken, and there are three people left.
